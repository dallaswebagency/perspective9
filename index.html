02

Are your ads working with you, or against you?

Google and Meta count every visitor to your site, including repeat buyers and people already searching your brand name, and report the sale as their own. Brands spending hundreds of millions can absorb that noise. For a growing brand, it decides which side your ads are on.

Against you: the platforms take the wheel

  • ✕Campaigns on autopilot, with budget the only lever
  • ✕Platform ROAS (return on ad spend, as Google and Meta report it) as the scorecard, and no further questions while orders keep coming in
  • ✕Returning customers and brand searches counted as wins
  • ✕Spend goes up, new customer numbers don't

With you: your numbers take the wheel

  • ✓Decisions made on new customer cost, order value, margin and cash flow
  • ✓Platform reports used as a guide, checked against your own sales data
  • ✓Clean signals, so the platforms' automation learns from real first-time buyers
  • ✓Spend grows when new customers do

07

Is this a good fit?

Probably, if:

  • +You sell online, usually turning over $1M to $10M a year, and spend $10,000 or more a month on Google and Meta ads.
  • +Your ad dashboards say things are working, but your sales don't feel it.
  • +Spend keeps going up while new customer growth stays flat.
  • +You want one senior person who looks past the ad account, at tracking, product pages and how sales are followed up.
  • +You're willing to fix the basics first, even if the first month is slower.
Or:

A service business where one new customer is worth $2,000 or more in profit, spending at least $3,000 a month on Google, and tracking which leads become sales.

Probably not, if:

  • –Ad budget under $3,000 a month for lead generation, or under $10,000 for ecommerce.
  • –You need video shot and edited. Static ad creative is available, and video comes from your team or creators.
  • –You want SEO, email and social posting from the same supplier.
  • –You need big results in the first two weeks.
  • –You're pre-launch or dropshipping.
